The Missing Step Is Not More Intelligence

The strongest critique of this article is not that Demis Hassabis is wrong about AI. It is that extraordinary scientific capability does not, by itself, establish a timetable for general intelligence or the authority to deploy it.

Ben Cohen’s WSJ profile connects Hassabis’s scientific ambitions, a two-to-three-year AGI forecast, an Einstein-style originality challenge, AI-driven drug discovery and proposed frontier-model oversight. It acknowledges risk and biological complexity. But it is an Innovators profile, not an independent technical audit.

01 / Editorial reading

Give the achievement its full weight

Dismissing AlphaFold as hype would be a weak argument. EMBL-EBI describes high-confidence AlphaFold2 predictions as often closely matching experimental structures, while explaining important limits. That is a genuine scientific contribution.

The precise lesson is that predictive AI can transform a bounded scientific workflow. That is much stronger than “AI is a gimmick,” and narrower than “general intelligence is nearly solved.” Past success earns a forecast serious attention. It does not supply the forecast’s missing measurements.

Original profile ↗ · ebi.ac.uk/training/…/how-accurate-are-alphafold-structure-predictions ↗

02 / Editorial reading

A timeline is not a countdown

DeepMind’s own cognitive-framework work acknowledges gaps in measuring general intelligence and proposes held-out tests and representative human baselines. That is a useful corrective to treating a date as a measured engineering schedule.

For the forecast to become decision-useful, we need the operational definition, the remaining capability gaps, allowed tools and retries, the human-intervention budget, and evidence that would move the forecast later.

An Einstein-style challenge is fascinating. It would also need a defensible historical training boundary, contamination controls, a novelty standard and independent evaluation. Reconstructing a scientific breakthrough could be impressive without establishing the entire cognitive range implied by AGI.

Original profile ↗ · blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/measuring-agi-cognitive-framework ↗

03 / Editorial reading

A better prediction is not yet a better treatment

Isomorphic’s September update describes predictive models, molecular-design agents, wet-lab results and preclinical progress toward clinical development. Those are meaningful intermediate outcomes, not demonstrated patient benefit.

The question is which handoffs improve: prediction to synthesis, synthesis to measured behavior, measured behavior to a viable candidate, and candidate to actual benefit. Ask for failures as well as successes, prospective evaluation and relevant baselines. Those are standards for evaluating the promise, not evidence that the company has failed them.

AI may accelerate hypothesis generation without eliminating the need to test the hypotheses.

Original profile ↗ · isomorphiclabs.com/articles/building-a-new-path-to-make-medicines-with-ai ↗

04 / Editorial reading

Good intentions are not an oversight system

DeepMind does have published safety machinery: capability thresholds, evaluations, mitigations and safety-case reviews. Ignoring that would manufacture an easy opponent.

The harder question remains: who can make a refusal binding when commercial incentives favor proceeding? Who appoints evaluators, controls funding, obtains necessary evidence, publishes adverse findings and stops deployment?

Industry expertise is necessary. Industry funding alone proves neither capture nor independence. The enforceable distribution of power matters more than the reassuring language around it.

Original profile ↗ · deepmind.google/blog/strengthening-our-frontier-safety-framework ↗

05 / Editorial reading

GhostMesh’s take

Intelligence, authority and verified outcomes require different evidence. A better model should not silently gain broader permissions. A successful tool invocation should not automatically become a successful mission.

An execution record should preserve the owner’s intent, the specific authority, resource and recipient boundaries, what actually changed, independent outcome evidence, and recovery behavior. Those distinctions must survive handoffs, retries, stale approvals and partial failures.

That is an architectural argument, not a claim that GhostMesh has already solved it. Our own stalled handoffs are a useful counterexample: we have competent models and passing tests, yet still struggle to carry approved work into a verified result. More intelligence alone does not close that gap.

Bottom line: take the capability seriously, keep the forecast conditional, make authority explicit and verify the outcome. Scientific optimism and operational discipline are not enemies. If powerful AI is arriving quickly, the need for reliable boundaries grows with it.
