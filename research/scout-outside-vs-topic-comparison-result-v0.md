# Scout outside-vocabulary versus familiar-topic editorial comparison

Status: owner-private assistant editorial assessment; **not** independently human-labeled recall, source originality scoring, or collaborator identity verification.

Input: 59 public-source lead observations. Scout's declared topical dictionary matched 9 and left 50 outside. The deterministic outside sample selected 12 across 11 source streams; this comparison pairs those twelve with all nine familiar-topic matches.

The filtered approach would hide the outside twelve, including:
- [SpecEvo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28706v1), already source-reviewed and provisionally **INVESTIGATE** in Scout, with independently unverified effectiveness.
- [Forging More Tasks into Better Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28815v1), primary abstract reviewed: Successive Scale Racing selects evidence-task combinations in agent harness evolution; reported benchmark effects are author claims. Provisional **INVESTIGATE**, no GhostMesh reproduction.

Other outside source links (e.g. GitHub's agent-scale Git infrastructure piece and Cloudflare WebMCP commerce) have only provisional source-identification reviews; do **not** count them as content-verified wins. Remaining leads are unreviewed.

The familiar cohort contains source-reviewed Agent Skills interoperability and VocabX specialization, both previously judged potentially relevant under bounded, evidence-first tests. The two cohorts are not randomized and have unequal maturity of prior assessment. Thus the 2/12 **is not a recall estimate, discovery yield, or validated relevance rate**.

Research takeaway: keep a source-balanced outside-topic exploration lane, but do not auto-promote or discard leads. The next decisive experiment is a blinded, independently labeled review of a fixed source sample, with consistent review budget, false-negative counts, surprise judgments, and documented source evidence.
