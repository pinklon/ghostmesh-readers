The Justice Department asked California and Los Angeles County to set aside ballots associated with a list of more than 20,000 people it described as **likely** noncitizens. Its [October 15 civil complaint, case 2:26-cv-12025](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1466286/dl), paragraphs 34–49 and prayer for relief, describes federal matching work and asks a court for ballot segregation pending eligibility checks. Paragraph 60 says DOJ could not investigate thousands of cases before the election. The filing establishes the government's allegation and requested remedy. It does not establish that all listed people are ineligible, that they voted unlawfully, or that the court granted relief.

## DOJ's strongest evidentiary case

The federal account deserves more than the shorthand “database match.” The complaint, paragraphs 33–43, pleads high-confidence identity links using multiple identifiers, checks for naturalization and derived citizenship, second-level review, and Homeland Security Investigations analysis and field work. An [October 19 memorandum supporting an ex parte TRO application, filed as ECF 22-2](https://electionlawblog.org/wp-content/uploads/gov.uscourts.cacd_.1043513.22.2.pdf), summarizes new declarations and says agencies narrowed more than 175,000 potential matches to more than 20,000 after further screens, manual work, and duplicate checks (pp. 20–23). It requests relief before the November 3 election (pp. 12–15). Those are the government's pleaded and declaration-based assertions. The declarations, underlying list, selection denominator, error rate, and voter-level proof were not independently inspected in this packet. The memorandum is a **motion**, not a signed court order. It also proves that a public search index describing October 15 as the latest filing is stale.

DOJ's sequence makes its leads potentially consequential. Yet a strong identity match is not the same proposition as current noncitizenship, a cast ballot, or an election official's intent. The complaint moves between “likely” leads and a stronger assertion that around 20,000 noncitizens are registered. The accessible filing does not provide a blinded validation sample by which we can measure either formulation. Its prayer, paragraphs 62–63, asks for declaratory and injunctive relief; it does not report that the judge granted either.

The recipients' records show two different kinds of access. In her [October 15 response](https://admin.cdn.sos.ca.gov/pdf/other/casos-to-doj-101526.pdf), pages 1–3, California Secretary of State Shirley Weber said her office had not received the underlying list or matching method, opposed ballot segregation on that basis, and offered to review supporting evidence. Los Angeles County said it did not open a USB drive delivered October 13 under its security protocols ([October 14 statement](https://content.lavote.gov/docs/rrcc/documents/20261014-statement_doj_letter-2.pdf), page 1). Its [October 15 counsel letter](https://rrcc.box.com/index.php?rm=box_download_shared_file&shared_name=y750a5njl1wiyksohn7aik9k2w3ilzkm&file_id=f_2528995513425), pages 1–4, says Homeland Security Investigations emailed an Excel copy October 14 and County staff reviewed it cursorily. County counsel said the file covered statewide addresses and lacked the method and individualized evidence needed for the requested County action. A cursory look is neither no access nor validation of the list. These are the recipients' accounts and contested legal positions, not an independent audit of DOJ's matching work.

In an [October 16 letter](https://www.democracydocket.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/DOJ-threatens-CA-official-with-criminal-exposure.pdf), pages 1–2, DOJ warned County counsel of possible criminal exposure for knowingly tabulating ballots associated with people it identified as noncitizens. The letter cites [18 U.S.C. § 611](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t18/s611/) and [§ 2](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t18/s2/), while still describing the list as likely matches. The warning is not a charge against an election official or a judicial finding that the statutes apply to any official's conduct.

## The countercase and the unresolved legal bridge

Four voter-rights organizations gave the ballot-side risk a direct voice in their [October 16 motion to intervene, ECF 15](https://naacp.org/sites/default/files/documents/U.S.%20v.%20California%20et%20al.%20-%20NAACP%20and%20LULAC%20Mot.%20to%20Intervene%20%5B10.16.26%5D%20%5Bas-filed%5D.pdf), pp. 17–19. The LULAC and NAACP groups argue that segregation could keep valid ballots from being counted and that outdated records can misidentify naturalized citizens. This verifies what the proposed intervenors argued, not that any specific list entry is wrong, that intervention was granted, or that the court accepted their position. Their examples and declarations do not supply a measured error rate for this list.

The Secretary's letter, page 3, also argues that an October 9 District of Columbia ruling against a separate federal voter-roll program may affect DOJ's demand. DOJ contests that interpretation in its [October 16 stay motion in the D.C. case, ECF 57](https://www.democracydocket.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/57-2026-10-16-DOJs-motion-for-stay-pending-appeal.pdf), pp. 13–14: it says DHS bought California's public voter file and did not use the SAVE process at issue there. That is a material counter to treating the earlier ruling as an automatic answer in California. Neither party's motion decides what the California court will hold.

The underlying legal distinction matters. Section 611 prohibits covered noncitizen voting in federal elections, while § 2 concerns aiding and abetting. DOJ argues ballot processing despite its warning can fall within federal law; California and the County argue the requested hold conflicts with voting rights, their administrative duties, and list-maintenance constraints. Whether a particular official's conduct satisfies a criminal statute, and whether this particular ballot remedy is lawful, require facts and legal rulings the letters do not provide. Wrongly holding an eligible ballot and counting an unlawful ballot are both consequential harms. The record here establishes competing risks, not the scale of either.

**After the October 17 episode:** DOJ [announced October 19](https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/11-foreign-nationals-arrested-california-federal-charges-alleging-they-illegally) that 28 people faced charges, 11 had been arrested, and all 28 appeared on the disputed list. These are prosecutorial allegations about listed individuals, not convictions, proof about the remaining entries, or charges against election officials. The charging instruments were not inspected. DOJ's assertion that California “refused to look” needs the distinction in County counsel's October 15 letter: County staff say they cursorily examined the emailed copy while declining DOJ's requested verification and segregation.

These cases are a serious reason not to dismiss every federal lead. They are not a validation study of the entire list: prosecution cases are selected, the individual charging papers were not inspected here, and the release offers no random sample or count of false matches. The alleged conduct concerns earlier registration, voting, or naturalization paperwork, not a proved ballot cast in the coming November election. The [DOJ-wide October 19 release](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-charges-28-foreign-nationals-california-election-crimes) expressly treats the charges as allegations and says defendants are presumed innocent. The 28 charged and 11 arrested counts should be taken from these releases; ECF 22-2 uses an inconsistent “28 arrested” reference on page 12.

## GhostMesh's take

The editorial rule is to keep four stages separate: **an investigative lead, verified individual eligibility, lawful ballot handling, and criminal responsibility for a named official**. DOJ has described a layered investigative process and later announced selected charges. That is stronger evidence for potentially valid leads than an unsupported assertion. It is still insufficient here to classify the full list as confirmed unlawful voters or the County's refusal as a proven crime. California's warning about disenfranchisement is a material risk, but it does not establish that every lead is mistaken. Neither side's advocacy should be made to do the work of an eligibility finding or court order.

This distinction has a GhostMesh product consequence: an evidence-led article should display a *requested injunction* as a request, an *intervention motion* as a motion, and a *criminal charge* as an allegation. Changing any of those labels to “verified” merely because a stamped filing or press release exists would mislead the reader. No software or policy change is asserted by this draft.

**What would change the call:** A signed California order would establish the court's current directive. Original criminal complaints, later dispositions, and a reviewable sample of list outcomes could strengthen or weaken DOJ's identification claim. Evidence about actual ballot handling and a court ruling on the statutory theory could change the assessment of the threat to officials. This analysis should be revised with dated sources when any of those arrive.
