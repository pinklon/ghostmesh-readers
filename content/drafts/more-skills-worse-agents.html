When More Agent Skills Make Agents Worse

David Ondrej · Source analysisNovember 9, 2026 · 42:25 video

The risk is not how many skills an agent owns. It is what the agent sees, chooses, trusts and invokes.

Source: Watch the original 42:25 video. This editorial draft uses a verified ReasonPack and machine-ASR transcript, not manually audited video.

The brief

Creator’s thesis: Prune most copied agent skills and keep a few relevant workflows.

Evidence boundary: A useful caution about discovery and context, but no demonstrated universal deletion percentage.

GhostMesh interpretation: Measure visible metadata, invocation errors, criticality and scope before retiring skills.

Skill count is not context cost

At 01:42 Ondrej separates skill descriptions from full bodies. At 02:09 he warns that even descriptions consume attention at scale. Anthropic documents progressive loading; Codex likewise has bounded discovery. The cost depends on what the harness exposes, not how many files exist.

The selection problem

At 03:00 the creator criticizes broad descriptions. Ambiguous metadata can make the wrong skill attractive. The correct measure is selection precision and false invocations, not a raw catalog-size limit.

Rare does not mean useless

At 11:18 and 14:19 he recommends pruning unused skills. That is a sensible inventory prompt, not a universal 80–90% deletion target. Incident recovery, compliance and security skills may be critical precisely because they are rarely needed.

A skill is not permission

At 29:00 he compares skills to SOPs. Reusable procedures can improve consistency, but a markdown skill cannot authorize privileged actions or replace an approval gate.

Scope before scale

At 33:40 and 36:56 he discusses portability and global scope. The transferable architecture principle is to expose relevant instructions on the right execution surface and avoid broad global injection.

Reuse needs provenance

At 39:00 the creator advocates team-shared skills. Safe reuse also requires ownership, versioning, trust boundaries and review.
